Census population, 1903 – 2024

Roxas City grew from 21,472 (1903) to 185,236 (2024) — about 8.6× in 121 years.
Source: PSA censuses of population (1903–2024) via PhilAtlas city profile;2 2024 count from the 2024 POPCEN.1

Annual population growth rate

Growth has cooled to below 1% per year in the 2020–2024 period.
Source: PSA, intercensal annualized growth rates.2 2024 rate (0.79%) per 2024 POPCEN summary.15

Roxas City hosts 22.18% of Capiz's 835,098 residents17 on 3.66% of its land.2
Computed: 185,236 ÷ 835,098 (PSA 2024 POPCEN).1,17

Age structure (2015 census)

Median age 25.56 · total dependency ratio 54.1 (youth 44.8, old-age 9.3). The 15–19 bracket is the largest single group.
Source: PSA, 2015 Census of Population age distribution for Roxas City.2 City-level age tables from the 2020/2024 censuses were not yet published in the cited series.

Households

Households nearly tripled since 1990 while average household size fell from 5.49 to 4.40 members (2015).2
Source: PSA census household counts, 1990–2015;2 2024 household count (43,257) from the 2024 POPCEN summary.15

Ten most populous barangays (2020)

Lawa-an and Baybay each hold over 9,000 residents; the city has 47 barangays.2
Source: PSA, 2020 Census of Population and Housing, barangay-level counts.2

Key demographic facts

  • Most populous in Capiz, second in Western Visayas after Iloilo City.15
  • 1,948 inhabitants per km² in 2024 — the city holds 22.18% of Capiz's residents on just 3.66% of the province's land area.1,17,2
  • Electorate: 112,738 registered voters for the May 2025 elections (up from 104,264 in 2019 — 54,036 female / 50,228 male then).15,2
  • Geography: coastal component city on Panay Island, 95.07 km², 7.0 m elevation, 47 barangays, postal code 5800.15,2

City Income

Where the city government's money comes from, how Roxas City is classified, and how household poverty has moved.

FY 2025 budget COA FY 2024 audit

FY 2025 budget — where the money comes from

The city is operating on a ₱1.43 billion budget for fiscal year 2025; more than ₱1 billion is its national tax allotment (NTA) share, while ₱413.9 million comes from local taxes and other revenues. ₱60.5 million is earmarked for Local Economic Enterprises (fishing port, market, slaughterhouse, transport terminal).10
Source: The Capiz Times, "P1.43 billion budget for City Hall," 25 January 2025.10 NTA slice (≈₱1.016 B) is derived: ₱1.43 B − ₱413.9 M.

City income class under RA 11964

Classified by average annual regular income over three fiscal years. The DOF reclassified Roxas City from third to second class in December 2024, and PSA's PSGC also lists "2nd" as the city's income class.11,12,1
Source: RA 11964 city income ranges (Supreme Court E-Library);13 reclassification reported by Manila Bulletin and Daily Guardian, Dec 2024.11,12

Annual regular income, FY 2009 – FY 2016

Locally sourced revenue + IRA/NTA + national tax shares: ₱368.7 M (2009) → ₱624.7 M (2016), a compound growth of about 7.8% per year. Growth on this series is what moved the city up the income ladder — regular income has since crossed ₱1 billion (2nd-class band).2,11
Source: BLGF annual regular income data for Roxas City, 2009–2016, via PhilAtlas.2 Compound growth computed by this page.

Methodology & Sources

What is measured, by whom, and how to read these charts.

Notes on the data

  • Census counts are de jure PSA census results. The 2024 figure comes from the 2024 Census of Population (POPCEN 2024); city-level age tables for that round were not in the cited series, so age structure uses the 2015 Census.2
  • CMCI covers LGUs that submit data; rankings compare Roxas City only against other component cities (116 in 2024, 114 in 2023). Higher score = more competitive; rank 1 = best. Pillar scores are published by DTI; the overall totals shown here are the arithmetic sum of the five pillar scores, as CMCI defines overall competitiveness as that sum.5,6
  • Income class is set by the DOF from the average annual regular income of three fiscal years, under RA 11964 (automatic income classification). Second class = ₱1.0 B–₱1.3 B; first class = ₱1.3 B and above.13
  • Poverty incidence is the proportion of the population below the official poverty threshold, estimated by PSA from the FIES via small area estimation; city estimates are not available every year.3,4
  • Provenance: the financial-figure summary for FY 2024 (revenue, expenditure, liabilities) is a compiled LGU profile whose figures are drawn from COA audit reports; total assets are cross-checked directly against the COA FY 2024 audit record.14,15
  • Rounding & derived values are flagged in each chart caption; shares and compound growth are computed by this page from the cited primary figures.
